What size dumpster do you need?

Dumpster sizes for every job

Four sizes cover most jobs in Indiana. Small jobs need a 10-yard box. Big jobs need a 40-yard box. The 20-yard box is the most rented size. Pick by debris type first, then by volume.

10-yard dumpster

A 10-yard box measures about 12 to 14 feet long, 8 feet wide, and 3.5 to 4 feet high. It holds about 50 to 70 bags. It fits a small cleanout, one room, or a small bathroom remodel. It also fits heavy debris like concrete, dirt, or brick. Heavy loads hit weight limits fast, so a small box is the safe pick. See the 10-yard dumpster page for full details.

20-yard dumpster

A 20-yard box measures about 22 feet long, 8 feet wide, and 4 to 4.5 feet high. It holds about 110 to 130 bags. It fits a kitchen remodel, a multi-room cleanout, new flooring, or a roofing tear-off. It is the most rented size in the country. Most homeowners start here. See the 20-yard dumpster page for full details.

30-yard dumpster

A 30-yard box measures about 22 feet long, 8 feet wide, and 6 feet high. It holds about 170 to 190 bags. It fits a whole-house cleanout, a major renovation, or a large construction job. Do not use it for heavy debris. Heavy material fills the weight limit long before the box fills up. See the 30-yard dumpster page for full details.

40-yard dumpster

A 40-yard box measures about 22 feet long, 8 feet wide, and 7 to 8 feet high. It holds about 230 to 250 bags. It fits major demolition, large construction, and light bulky debris. Never load it with heavy material. Concrete and dirt belong in a small box. See the 40-yard dumpster page for full details.

Permits and what can go in

A dumpster on your own driveway usually needs no permit. A dumpster on a street, sidewalk, alley, or right-of-way usually needs a city permit. This rule holds in every Indiana city source we checked.

In Indianapolis, street placement needs a Right-of-Way Use Permit from the City of Indianapolis. We do not name a department here, because two sources name different departments. In Fort Wayne, street placement needs a Right-of-Way Occupancy and Barricade Permit. The permit comes from the City of Fort Wayne Division of Public Works. In South Bend, the city asks for at least three business days to process a container permit. In Evansville, no Evansville rule was found, so check with the City of Evansville Public Works before street placement. Read the full Indiana permit guide for the city by city rules.

Indiana waste rules come from IDEM, the Indiana Department of Environmental Management. Waste must go to a permitted facility. Open dumping and open burning are illegal in Indiana. Landfills must not accept hazardous waste, whole tires, lead-acid batteries, electronics, or untreated infectious waste. Keep liquids, paint, oil, gasoline, chemicals, asbestos, and appliances with refrigerant still inside out of the box. What can I put in a dumpster?

You can load household debris, wood, drywall, flooring, and yard waste. You cannot load hazardous waste, liquids, paint, chemicals, whole tires, lead-acid batteries, or electronics. Indiana landfills bar these items under IDEM rules.

Will a dumpster damage my driveway?

Steel rails can mark or crack a weak driveway. Plywood boards under the rails spread the weight and help protect the surface. Ask about driveway protection when you book.
