Delivery and pickup

You must give a clear path for the truck. The driver needs 10.5 feet wide by 60 feet straight clearance, and 25 to 30 feet of vertical clearance. Move cars, bins, and low branches before delivery. Mark the drop spot if the driver needs a guide. If the truck cannot reach the spot, delivery may fail. A failed delivery may carry a fee. [TO CONFIRM] Pickup works the same way. Keep the path clear on pickup day. Keep the load level with the top edge. Do not overfill the box. An overfilled box may not be picked up until you level it.

Placement and permits

A dumpster on your own driveway usually needs no permit. A dumpster on a street, sidewalk, alley, or right-of-way usually needs a city permit. You must get that permit before delivery day unless we agree otherwise. [TO CONFIRM] Check your HOA rules before delivery if your home has an HOA. Tell us about narrow driveways, steep grades, soft ground, or shared access before delivery day.

Prohibited items

Do not load banned items. The banned list covers hazardous waste, liquids, paint, oil, gasoline, chemicals, asbestos, and medical or infectious waste. It also covers whole tires, lead-acid batteries, electronics, and appliances with refrigerant still inside. Indiana landfills bar these items under IDEM rules. Open dumping and open burning are illegal in Indiana. If banned items are found in your load, you may face extra fees or removal costs. [TO CONFIRM] Heavy debris like concrete, dirt, brick, and tile must go in a small box only. Fill heavy loads only to the marked line.

This term is to confirm with the business. Do not treat it as final. Steel rails and heavy loads can mark, crack, or sink a driveway. This risk is higher on old concrete, thin asphalt, pavers, and soft ground. Plywood boards under the rails can help spread the weight.
