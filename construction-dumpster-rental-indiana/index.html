Quick answer

A construction dumpster takes lumber, drywall, flooring, siding, and roofing waste. Most jobsite jobs use a 20-yard, 30-yard, or 40-yard box. A standard rental runs 7 days, with swaps when the box fills. Heavy debris needs its own small box, not a big one.

  • The job is one small repair with only a few bags.

What debris goes in a construction box

General construction waste fits. Wood, drywall, shingles, siding, flooring, metal, and packaging all go in. Concrete, brick, dirt, and tile need a separate heavy-material box.

Some items stay out of every box. Paint, solvents, oil, fuel, chemicals, and asbestos stay out. So do tires, batteries, electronics, and appliances with refrigerant. IDEM rules bar whole tires and lead-acid batteries from Indiana landfills.

Never mix heavy debris into a general box. A 30-yard box full of concrete can weigh over 10 tons. The truck cannot haul it, and the load gets refused. Use a 10-yard lowboy for heavy material.

Yard waste and other materials we haul

A yard waste box takes branches, limbs, leaves, grass, brush, and small stumps. It also takes garden waste and hedge trimmings. Keep the load to plant material only.

Indiana keeps yard waste out of landfill disposal. Yard waste goes to a compost site instead. The Indiana Department of Environmental Management, IDEM, sets this rule.

Yard waste is light, so weight is rarely the problem. Volume is the problem. Branches fill a box fast. Cut them short and pack them tight. Cut long branches down first. Long limbs waste space and poke over the rim.

Some items stay out of a yard box. Treated wood, fencing, and deck boards are construction waste, not yard waste. Paint, oil, chemicals, and household trash stay out too. Dirt is heavy and needs a concrete lowboy, not a yard box.

A 10-yard box fits one tree or a seasonal pruning. It holds about 3 pickup loads. A 20-yard box fits a large yard cleanup. It holds about 6 pickup loads. A 30-yard box fits a full lot clearout or storm cleanup. It holds about 9 pickup loads.

Homeowners use yard boxes in spring and fall. Spring brings pruning and bed clearouts. Fall brings leaves by the bag. Storm cleanup needs a bigger box. Fallen limbs fill space fast. Book early after a storm, since routes fill fast.

What size fits a construction job?

A 20-yard box suits a small remodel or a roofing tear-off. It holds about 6 pickup loads and 110 to 130 bags. Its weight limit usually runs 2 to 4 tons.

A 30-yard box suits a major renovation or a large build. It holds about 9 pickup loads and 170 to 190 bags. Its weight limit usually runs 3 to 5 tons.

A 40-yard box suits major demolition and large builds with light, bulky waste. It holds about 12 pickup loads and 230 to 250 bags. Its weight limit usually runs 4 to 6 tons. Never load a 40-yard box with heavy material.

Weight limits and overage fees

Weight beats volume on a jobsite. Shingles, tile, and plaster hit the limit while the box still looks half full. Plaster weighs about 3 times drywall per square foot. Wet drywall weighs about 4 times its dry weight.

Overage usually costs 85 to 155 dollars per ton across Indiana. The Indianapolis live rate is 155 dollars per ton. Across Indiana, overage runs 85 to 155 dollars per ton, Nov 2026.

Load heavy items first and low. Break long boards so they lie flat. Keep the load below the rim. A load over the rim can be refused at pickup, and that costs the crew a day.

Contractors and builders: keep the schedule tight

Order the next box before the first one fills. A crew with no box stops work or piles debris on the ground. A standing swap schedule beats a last-minute call.

Put one person in charge of the box. That person checks the fill line each day. That person also keeps banned items out. One banned item can mean a fee of 100 dollars or more, or a refused load.

Share gate codes and site hours at booking. A driver who cannot reach the box charges a trip fee in most markets, often 50 to 150 dollars. Clear access saves that money.

Property managers: renovation and turnover work

Property managers use construction boxes for unit turns and roof jobs. One box per building keeps debris off the lot. Tenants see a clean site, not a pile.

Street placement needs a city permit in most Indiana cities. A driveway or private lot usually needs none. Check with the city Public Works office before you book a street spot.

Plan the pickup around the next trade. Roofers need the box gone before the gutters go on. Painters need it gone before the final walk. Set the call date at booking.

Frequently asked questions

Can I get a swap when the construction dumpster fills?

Yes, a swap brings an empty box and hauls the full one away. Ask about swap timing and charges at booking, so the crew keeps working. [TO CONFIRM]

Can concrete go in a construction dumpster?

No, not in a general box. Concrete, dirt, and brick need a dedicated 10-yard heavy-material box. A big box full of concrete weighs too much to haul.

How long can I keep a construction dumpster?

A standard rental runs 7 days. Extensions usually cost 15 to 40 dollars per day across Indiana. Our exact rate is confirmed at booking. [TO CONFIRM]

Do I need a permit for a jobsite dumpster?

A box on private land usually needs no permit. A box on a street, sidewalk, or alley almost always needs a city permit. In Indianapolis, street placement needs a Right-of-Way Use Permit from the City of Indianapolis.

What happens if the crew overfills the box?

The driver can refuse pickup until the load sits below the rim. The fill line sits about 6 inches below the rim. Keep debris level and call early for a swap.

Can grass and leaves go in a yard waste dumpster?

Yes, grass, leaves, and brush all go in. Keep the load to plant material only. No household trash, no treated wood, and no soil mixed in.

Can I put dirt in a yard waste dumpster?

No, not in a yard waste box. Dirt is heavy and needs a concrete lowboy. A yard box is for light plant material only.

Can stumps go in a yard waste dumpster?

Small stumps can go in. Cut them down and split them first. A huge stump wastes space, so ask at booking if yours fits. [TO CONFIRM]
