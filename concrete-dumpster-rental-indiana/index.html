Quick answer

A concrete box takes concrete, brick, dirt, asphalt, and tile. It must be a 10-yard lowboy in most cases. A 30-yard box full of concrete can weigh over 10 tons, and no truck can haul it. Keep the load clean, with no household trash mixed in.

How concrete dumpster rental Indiana works

Book a 10-yard lowboy for the heavy load. The driver sets it close to the work. Crews load concrete, brick, or dirt straight in. You call when it hits the weight limit.

Weight decides when the box is full. The box may look half full and still be at its limit. Stop loading when the weight is reached, not when the box looks full.

The truck needs 10.5 feet of width and 60 feet of straight space. It needs 25 to 30 feet of clear height. A lowboy sits low, so it loads easy with a wheelbarrow.

When a Concrete Dumpster Is Wrong for You

  • The load mixes concrete with wood, drywall, or trash.
  • You plan to fill a 20-yard or 30-yard box with concrete.
  • The job is a full driveway and you booked only one box.
  • The drop spot is soft lawn with no firm ground.

What a concrete box holds

A concrete box takes broken concrete, brick, block, dirt, asphalt, and tile. These are clean heavy materials. They go to a different stream than household waste.

Keep the load clean. No wood, no drywall, and no household trash mixed in. Mixed loads can be refused or charged more at the landfill.

Some items stay out of every box. Paint, oil, fuel, chemicals, and asbestos stay out. So do tires, batteries, and electronics. IDEM rules bar whole tires and lead-acid batteries from Indiana landfills.

Why a 10-yard box is the right size

A 10-yard box is about 12 to 14 feet long and 8 feet wide. It stands 3.5 to 4 feet high. It holds about 3 pickup loads. Its weight limit usually runs 1 to 3 tons.

Concrete weighs about 150 pounds per cubic foot. A full 10-yard box of concrete can weigh far more than its limit. Load it only partway and stop at the weight cap.

A bigger box does not help with heavy debris. A 20-yard or 30-yard box full of concrete breaks the truck limit. Use two small boxes instead of one big one.

Contractors: plan the heavy haul

Contractors break concrete in stages. Load one box, call for pickup, and start the next. A staged plan keeps the crew working and the site clear.

Keep rebar and wire mesh out when you can. Ask at booking what the load rules allow. [TO CONFIRM] Clean loads move faster at the landfill.

Mark the drop spot on flat ground. A loaded lowboy is heavy. Soft ground can sink under it. Pick a paved spot or a firm, level area.

Homeowners: patio, driveway, and tile jobs

Homeowners use concrete boxes for patios, driveways, and sidewalks. A small patio fits one lowboy in most cases. A full driveway needs two or more.

Break the slab into pieces one person can lift. Big chunks waste space and hide weight. Small pieces pack tight and load safe.

Keep dirt separate from concrete when you can. Mixed heavy loads still work, but a clean load is the simplest load. Ask at booking if your mix is fine. [TO CONFIRM]

Frequently asked questions

Can I put concrete in a 20-yard dumpster?

No, not a full load. A 20-yard box full of concrete weighs too much to haul. Use a 10-yard lowboy for concrete, brick, and dirt.

How much concrete fits in a 10-yard dumpster?

Less than you think. Concrete weighs about 150 pounds per cubic foot. A 10-yard box hits its weight limit long before it looks full.

Can dirt and concrete go in the same box?

Yes, in most cases, when both are clean heavy material. Keep wood, drywall, and household trash out. Ask at booking if your mix is fine. [TO CONFIRM]

Can I put tile in a concrete dumpster?

Yes, tile is a clean heavy material. It goes in the same lowboy as concrete and brick. Keep grout dust and thinset in the load, not loose on the ground.

How long can I keep a concrete dumpster?

A standard rental runs 7 days. Most concrete jobs finish in a few days. Extensions usually cost 15 to 40 dollars per day across Indiana. [TO CONFIRM]
