Quick answer

A commercial dumpster suits office fit-outs, retail refits, warehouse clearouts, and multi-unit turns. Most commercial jobs use a 20-yard, 30-yard, or 40-yard box. Boxes sit on private lots in most cases, so no street permit is needed. Swaps keep long projects moving.

How commercial dumpster rental Indiana works

Book a box for the project dates. The driver sets it in the lot or loading area. Staff load it as the work goes on. You call for pickup or a swap when it fills.

Long projects run on a swap rhythm. A full box leaves and an empty box takes its place. Set the rhythm at booking, so no crew waits for a box.

Delivery needs clear truck access. The truck needs 10.5 feet of width and 60 feet of straight space. It needs 25 to 30 feet of clear height. Keep the dock and lot clear on delivery day.

  • The box must sit on a public street with no permit.

What a commercial box holds

Office and retail waste fits. Old desks, shelving, carpet, ceiling tiles, drywall, and packaging all go in. Warehouse clearouts add pallets, racking, and broken stock.

Some items stay out. Paint, solvents, oil, fuel, and chemicals stay out. So do tires, batteries, electronics, and appliances with refrigerant. IDEM rules bar whole tires and lead-acid batteries from Indiana landfills.

Electronics need a separate route. Indiana bans electronics from landfills under the IDEM E-Cycle programme. Old monitors and computers go to an e-waste drop-off, not the box.

What size fits a commercial job?

A 20-yard box suits a small office refit or a single retail unit. It holds about 6 pickup loads and 110 to 130 bags. Its weight limit usually runs 2 to 4 tons.

A 30-yard box suits a full floor refit or a multi-unit turn. It holds about 9 pickup loads and 170 to 190 bags. Its weight limit usually runs 3 to 5 tons.

A 40-yard box suits a warehouse clearout or a full demolition with light, bulky waste. It holds about 12 pickup loads and 230 to 250 bags. Its weight limit usually runs 4 to 6 tons. Never load it with heavy material.

Weight limits and overage fees

Commercial loads get heavy fast. Carpet, ceiling tile, and wet debris add up. A box can pass its weight limit while it still looks half full.

Market data puts overage at 85 to 155 dollars per ton across Indiana. The Indianapolis live rate is 155 dollars per ton. Across Indiana, overage runs 85 to 155 dollars per ton, Nov 2026.

Keep a tarp over the box in rain. Wet carpet and drywall soak up water and weight. Cover the load each night, and load heavy items low and flat.

Property managers: turns, refits, and cleanouts

Property managers use commercial boxes for turns between tenants. One box clears a unit or a floor. It holds furniture, carpet, fixtures, and debris in one load.

Time the box to the lease gap. Drop it the day the old tenant leaves. Pick it up the day before the new tenant moves in. A box left in the lot blocks parking and draws complaints.

Check HOA and lease rules before you book. Some sites ban street placement. Some limit how long a box can sit. Share the rules with us at booking.

Business owners: fit-outs without the mess

Fit-outs make more waste than owners expect. Old counters, shelving, and flooring fill a box fast. A 30-yard box covers most full-store refits.

Put the box where staff can reach it without crossing customers. A rear lot or loading dock works best. Keep the front clear and the site safe.

Name one staff contact for the driver. That person opens gates and clears the route. A missed drop costs a trip fee in most markets, often 50 to 150 dollars.

Frequently asked questions

Can a business schedule repeat swaps?

Yes, long projects run on a swap rhythm. A full box leaves and an empty box takes its place. Set the rhythm and the rate in writing at booking.

Can electronics go in a commercial dumpster?

No. Indiana bans electronics from landfills under the IDEM E-Cycle programme. Send old computers and monitors to an e-waste drop-off instead.

How long can I keep a commercial dumpster?

Seven days are included as standard. Extensions usually cost 15 to 40 dollars per day across Indiana. Our exact rate is confirmed at booking.

Do I need a permit for a box in my lot?

A box on private land usually needs no permit. A box on a street or sidewalk almost always needs a city permit. Check with your city Public Works office first.

What if my load goes over the weight limit?
