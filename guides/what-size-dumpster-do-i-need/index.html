What Size Dumpster Do I Need?

What size dumpster do I need for your job? Most Indiana homes need a 20 yard, but heavy debris changes the answer, so check weight before volume.

Quick answer

A 10 yard fits a small cleanout or one room. A 20 yard fits a kitchen remodel or a multi room cleanout. A 30 yard fits a whole house cleanout. A 40 yard fits major demolition. Heavy debris like concrete or shingles needs a small box, because weight runs out before space does.

The quick rule for picking a size

Start with the job, not the box. List what you will throw away. Then check two numbers: volume and weight. Volume is how much space the debris fills. Weight is how much it weighs on the scale.

Light bulky waste fills volume first. Furniture, boxes, and yard waste fall in this group. Heavy waste hits the weight limit first. Concrete, dirt, brick, tile, and shingles fall in that group. A 30 yard full of concrete can weigh 10 tons or more. No standard truck can haul that legally.

When you sit between two sizes, size up. The cost gap between sizes is small. The cost of a second haul is large. Most homeowners underestimate by one size, per sizing guides from Budget Dumpster and Jiffy Junk.

What size dumpster do I need when debris is heavy?

Every size has a weight limit, not just a volume limit. A 30 yard holds more space than a 20 yard. Its weight rating is often the same. That is the trap. Fill a 30 yard with shingles and you can pass the limit while the box looks half full.

Shingles weigh about 250 pounds per square. A 2,000 square foot roof can produce 3,000 to 7,000 pounds of tear off waste. Plaster weighs about three times drywall per square foot. Wet drywall weighs about four times its dry weight. Plan for the wettest, heaviest version of your debris.

Concrete and dirt need a dedicated heavy debris box. Most haulers forbid heavy material in large boxes. A 10 yard lowboy is the right call. It keeps the load legal and the bill low.

Three mistakes to avoid

First, do not rent the smallest box to save money. A second haul costs more than the size upgrade. Second, do not load heavy debris in a big box. The weight fee will wipe out any saving. Third, do not guess the weight of a roof. Count the squares, count the layers, and size up if you are close to the limit.

Photograph the empty dumpster when it arrives. If debris is already inside, tell us at once. You should not pay for weight you did not load. This advice comes from the Dumpsters.com FAQ.

Frequently asked questions

What is the most common dumpster size for a home cleanout?

A 20 yard is the most rented size. It holds about 6 pickup loads, or 110 to 130 bags. It fits a kitchen remodel, a multi room cleanout, and most flooring jobs.

What size fits a garage cleanout?

A 10 yard usually fits a garage, attic, or basement cleanout. It holds about 3 pickup loads. Size up to a 20 yard if the garage holds large furniture.

What size fits a roof tear-off?

A one layer tear off of 1,500 to 2,500 square feet usually fits a 10 to 20 yard. A two layer tear off needs a 20 to 30 yard. Shingles weigh about 250 pounds per square, so weight decides first.

Can I put concrete in a 30 yard dumpster?

No. Concrete, dirt, brick, and tile need a small heavy debris box, usually a 10 yard lowboy. A large box full of concrete can weigh 10 tons or more and cannot be hauled legally.

What happens if I pick a dumpster that is too small?

You pay for a second haul or an empty and return swap. That costs more than renting one size up at the start. When you sit between sizes, choose the bigger one.

How much space does delivery need?

Delivery needs 10.5 feet wide by 60 feet of straight clearance, plus 25 to 30 feet of vertical clearance. Move vehicles, unlock gates, and check overhead wires before the truck arrives.
