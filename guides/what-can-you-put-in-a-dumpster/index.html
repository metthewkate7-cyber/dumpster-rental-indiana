What Can You Put in a Dumpster?

What can you put in a dumpster in Indiana? Most household junk, yard waste, and construction debris are fine. Indiana law bans tires, batteries, electronics, and hazardous waste from landfills.

Quick answer

Allowed: household junk, furniture, yard waste, wood, drywall, flooring, roofing, and general construction debris. Banned by Indiana law: whole tires, lead acid batteries, electronics, liquids, hazardous waste, asbestos, and appliances with refrigerant. One banned item can cost $100 or more, or stop pickup entirely.

Published: November 13, 2026.

What can you put in a dumpster: the allowed list

Most of what comes out of a home or a job site is allowed. Household junk leads the list. Furniture, mattresses in most markets, clothing, toys, and old appliances without refrigerant all go in. Yard waste is allowed in a general box in most cases. Branches, leaves, and grass count as yard waste.

Construction debris is allowed too. Wood, drywall, flooring, siding, cabinets, and roofing shingles all fit. Concrete, dirt, brick, and tile are allowed only in a dedicated heavy debris box. They are too heavy for a general box. Tell us the debris type when you book so the right box arrives.

Indiana law: what landfills must refuse

Indiana law sets the banned list, not the hauler. Waste must go to a permitted facility allowed by IDEM for that waste type. Open dumping and open burning of waste is illegal in Indiana. The rules sit in Indiana Administrative Code 329 IAC 10 for solid waste. Hazardous waste sits under 329 IAC 3.1.

Municipal solid waste landfills in Indiana must not accept hazardous waste. They must also refuse certain PCB waste, untreated infectious waste, and whole waste tires. Vegetative matter is also barred unless it comes from homes or is properly bagged. The IDEM page on landfills and land disposal units states these rules.

Whole tires carry their own state ban. Indiana Code 13-20-14-1 bars landfills from taking whole waste tires, with limited exemptions. Tire shops must take back one waste tire per new tire sold. Open dumping or burning of tires is illegal.

Electronics are banned from Indiana landfills under the IDEM E-Cycle program. Lead acid batteries may not go in Indiana landfills either. State law keeps yard waste out of landfill disposal. Take it to a compost site where that rule applies.

Where to take banned items in Indiana

Fort Wayne has a clear drop off point. The Allen County Household Hazardous Waste Facility sits at 2260 Carroll Road, Fort Wayne, IN 46818. It takes paint and other household hazardous waste. Check its hours before you drive there.

South Bend has one too. The Household Hazardous Waste Center sits at 1105 E. 5th Street, Mishawaka, IN 46544. St. Joseph County runs a permanent drop off, plus separate routes for electronics, mercury, sharps, and tires.

Indianapolis and Evansville drop off points were not named in our research. Check your county solid waste district or the IDEM site for the nearest facility. Do not put a banned item in the dumpster and hope no one notices.

What happens if a banned item is found

One banned item can cost $100 or more. Sorting fees for a mixed load can reach $100 to $500 or more. The driver can refuse pickup until you remove the item at your own cost. The load can be rejected at the transfer station or landfill, which doubles the hauling cost.

Extra charges can take up to 120 days to process, per Dumpsters.com. Landfill and driver reports arrive late. A fee can land months after the job ends. Keep banned items out from day one.

Mattresses deserve a special note. Rules vary by market. Some haulers take them, sometimes for a surcharge near $100 each. Some states ban them from landfills. Ask us about mattresses when you book.

Frequently asked questions

Can I put a mattress in a dumpster?

Rules vary by market. Some haulers accept mattresses, sometimes for a surcharge near $100 each. Ask us when you book so the answer is clear before delivery.

Can I put paint in a dumpster?

Liquid paint is banned. Dried latex paint is sometimes allowed. Take liquid paint, solvents, and oil to a household hazardous waste facility instead.

Can I put tires in a dumpster?

No. Indiana law bars landfills from taking whole waste tires under IC 13-20-14-1. Tire shops must take back one waste tire per new tire sold.

Can I put electronics in a dumpster?

No. Indiana bans electronics from landfills under the IDEM E-Cycle program. Use an e-waste drop off or a retailer take back program.

Can I put concrete in a general dumpster?

No. Concrete, dirt, brick, and tile need a dedicated heavy debris box, usually a 10 yard lowboy. They are too heavy for a general debris box.

What happens if a banned item slips into my dumpster?

The driver can refuse pickup, or the load can be rejected at the landfill. Fees start near $100 per item and sorting fees can reach $500 or more. Remove banned items before pickup day.
